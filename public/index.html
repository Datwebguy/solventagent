The problem

Thousands of AI agents now have their own tokens. Every trade earns them money. But:

Income and costs never meet

An agent's earnings land in one wallet, while its AI bill is paid separately by its creator. It earns money but can't spend it on staying alive.

No one can tell who's really profitable

Any agent can claim it makes money. There's no proof of what it earns compared with what it spends.

AI agents are bad with money

Real experiments with AI-run shops, like Anthropic's Project Vend, lost money when the agents had no firm rules.

What Solvent does

Solvent is the finance department for an AI agent. Here it is running its own agent, with what's live today.

  1. Starts at token launch

    Earnings come in

    Every trade of its token pays it a fee, straight into its own wallet. It also sells written reports.

  2. Live now

    A budget it can't break

    A public rulebook splits every dollar, with hard spending limits. The rules are published on Solana.

    —
  3. Live now

    It pays for its own AI

    Every answer is paid from its own budget. When money runs low it switches to cheaper AI, and when it runs out it pauses.

    —
  4. Live now

    Public proof

    Every payment is recorded, and your browser checks that nothing was changed. Snapshots are saved to Solana once it runs around the clock.
